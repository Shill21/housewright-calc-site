The money pitch

Exact fractions, not rounded decimals.

Every other calculator on your phone converts to a decimal and rounds. Housewright Calc keeps the math in feet, inches, and fractions the whole way through — addition, subtraction, multiplication, division — down to 1/64″. When a value truly isn't exact, it says so instead of quietly rounding.

Unit Converter shows its work, too — 1 m ≈ 3′ 3-3/8″ — when a conversion doesn't land on an exact fraction, it's marked with ≈, not printed as if it were exact.

Questions a builder would ask
Does it really work offline?

Yes. Housewright Calc makes no network calls of its own — every calculator, drawing, and cut sheet runs entirely on your device. The only network activity involved anywhere is the App Store purchase itself, which Apple handles.

Do I need an account?

No. There are no user accounts. Your calculations, saved history, and settings live only on your device (and your own iCloud or iTunes backups, if you use them).

Is this a subscription?

No — and it never will be. The free tier is free forever. Pro is a single $29.99 purchase ($19.99 at launch) that unlocks everything else, for life, across your devices.

What's actually free?

The Dimensional Calculator (full, forever), the Unit & Material Converter, and three complete pro calculators — Framing, Concrete, and Rough Opening. One purchase unlocks the rest.

Does Family Sharing work?

Yes. The Pro unlock supports Family Sharing, so one purchase can cover your household.

I bought it — what if I switch phones?

Restore your purchase from the App Store. It's tied to your Apple ID, not to one installation, so it comes back on any device, for life — everything already purchased stays purchased.

Does it know my local building code?

It checks against IRC baselines — riser/tread limits, headroom, guard spacing, ramp slope — with code references shown on the drawing. Always verify local amendments; the app says so too.

Any ads or tracking?

No ads, ever, and no analytics or tracking of any kind. The privacy label is a clean "no data collected" — see the privacy policy for the full detail.
